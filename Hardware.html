sudo nano /etc/systemd/system/magneto.service

[Unit]

Description=Magneto Manager Service

After=network.target

[Service]

Type=simple

User=root

ExecStart=/bin/bash /home/pi/auto-uuid/magneto-run.sh

[Install]

WantedBy=multi-user.target

sudo apt-get install python3-pip

Extruder stepper motor

The nitehawk36 board delivers a sense_resistor of 0.100, so change it in your config

[tmc2209 extruder]
uart_pin: nhk:gpio0
interpolate: true
run_current: 1.0   #run and hold current are depending on motor you have

                               #Keep it on the original values if you don't change the motor

                               #Additional cooling (cooling fan e.g.) is necessary with current higher than 0.7
hold_current: 0.6
sense_resistor: 0.100
stealthchop_threshold: 0


Additional cooling fan

The stands on the magneto toolhead can fit a 40 x 40 cooling fan. It can be wired to nitehawk36 Probe port, using GND and PRB+. Its always on, so a quite fan is recommended (Noctua e.g.). Voltage can be set to 24 or 5V by jumper.

Bowden Tube

The positioning of the bowden tube in all versions of the Magneto X can cause issues:

  • For v1.0 and v1.5 printers, it can easily get tangled/block the movement of the extruder.
  • For v2.0 printers, the bowden tube was moved to be incorporated into the cable chain going to the print head but this introduced a lot of friction making it more likely to encounter filament grinding and other issues.

To fix these issues, there are a few different approaches that can be taken.

Before Modifying the Bowden Tube on v2.0 Printers

On v2.0 printers, you will want to remove the bowden tube from the cable chain leading to the print head. This can be done in one of two ways:

  1. The easiest: Buy a replacement bowden tube and cut the one in the cable chain flush with the cable chain. This leaves the now derelict bowden tube in the cable chain, but you also don't have to remove it from the cable chain which can be a bit finnicky.
  1. Carefully remove the bowden tube from the cable chain and reuse it. You will need to do this by removing it from the print head, then removing it from any zip ties and replacing those zip ties if other cables were relying on the zip ties, and then carefully pull it out of the cable chain.

Drilling the Acrylic Panels of the Enclosure

For some of these mods, you will need to drill a new hole through the enclosure for the bowden tube to enter from or widen an existing hole. Because the panels are acrylic, this can easily cause the panels to chip, crack, or in the worst case, break apart. Some tips to help prevent this are:

  • Use the correct drill bit, most wood and metal drill are not suited for drilling acrylic. That said, if you don't have access to a plastic drill bit, try to use a drill bit that has as much of an angle on it as possible and doesn't have a lip.
  • Put tape on both sides of where you are drilling. This will help reinforce the structural integrity of the acrylic. Even better, if you just got an enclosure, don't remove the film on the panels until you are done drilling as the film is much better than tape at preventing the acrylic from being damaged.
  • Go slowly, especially if you aren't using a drill bit suited for plastic. This will let you shave off a bit of plastic at a time and hopefully not bite into the acrylic too much which can cause cracking.
  • Use a support material below where you are drilling.
  • Use clamps, especially if you aren't using a plastic drill bit. Just don't clamp them too hard as excess pressure can crack the acrylic.

Realistically, unless you are really good at this, you will probably chip the acrylic at least a little bit if you don't have a plastic drill bit. You can find more information, including videos, on how to drill into acrylic at the Tap Plastics page for their plastic drill bits.

If you do crack or break the acrylic, you can easily fix this with some epoxy. Apply it to both sides of the damaged area/piece. To keep the epoxy as flush to the acrylic as possible, you can use wax paper to sandwich it.

Optimal Positioning of the Bowden Tube

  • If you have vertical space and can move where the roll of filament dispenses from, having the filament from above the printer is ideal
  • If you don't have vertical space, you want to guide the bowden tube to the center back of the printer before letting it freely go to the print head. This minimized the amount of bowden tube needed.

Using a Larger Bowden Tube

Because the Magneto X is a direct drive extruder, a larger bowden tube can be used to guide the filament to the extruder. This helps when using high friction filaments. However, the key problem when using a larger diameter bowden tube is that you will still need to step down to 2mm at the extruder.
